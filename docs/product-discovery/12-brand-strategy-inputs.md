# 12 — Brand strategy inputs

These are inputs to choose from, not a chosen identity. Facts are drawn from the product as built; everything else is labelled as a hypothesis or option.

## 1. What MatOS represents (fact-based)

- **An operating system for the mat:** the place a BJJ academy runs from (members, classes, attendance, belts, money, waivers, prospects), built around how jiu-jitsu gyms actually work (stripes, grading days, kids belts, open mats, drop-ins).
- **Built with a real gym, not a template:** the first customer is a local academy; features were prioritised from a flow audit of that gym's first weeks.
- **Respectful of data:** video never leaves the device, opponents' names are redacted on public shares, and every change is audited.
- **Admin that gets out of the way:** an assistant you can ask "who hasn't trained in three weeks?"

## 2. Possible brand personality (options)

| Option                      | Feels like                                | Fits because                                                                         | Risk                              |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| **The calm head coach**     | Steady, technical, encouraging, few words | Mirrors the product's tone rule ("terse and functional") and the coach as main buyer | Can read as plain                 |
| **The training partner**    | Friendly, on your side, a bit of humour   | Suits the athlete app and share cards                                                | Less authority for money matters  |
| **The black belt engineer** | Precise, data-driven, quietly expert      | Suits analysis, readiness, the assistant                                             | Can feel cold to small gym owners |

## 3. Perceptions to aim for

- _"Made by people who train."_
- _"It knows how a BJJ gym works."_ (stripes, kids belts, gradings)
- _"Less admin, more mat time."_
- _"My members' data is safe here."_
- _"It's fair for a small gym."_ (free to start, no upfront fees)

## 4. Brand attributes

Grounded, precise, respectful, efficient, community-minded, modern but not flashy.

## 5. Benefits

- **Functional:**
  - Onboard a gym in one session.
  - Know who's paid and who's due a stripe.
  - Check-ins that just work (phone, tablet, parent).
  - Waivers and GDPR handled.
  - Ask the assistant instead of digging.
  - Athletes see their own progress.
- **Emotional:**
  - Control without bureaucracy.
  - Pride in the academy's progression.
  - Members feel recognised (belts, streaks, share cards).
  - Peace of mind (audit, privacy).

## 6. Positioning statements (hypotheses to test)

1. _For independent BJJ academies, MatOS is the gym system built around the belt, so coaches spend less time on admin and more on the mat._
2. _MatOS connects running the gym with improving the athlete: attendance, gradings and match analysis in one place._
3. _The first BJJ gym software you can just ask._ (assistant-led; depends on the assistant being a reliable, affordable core feature)

## 7. Possible taglines (drafts)

- "Built for the mat."
- "Every stripe, accounted for."
- "Less admin. More rolls."
- "The OS for your mat."
- "From first class to black belt."

## 8. Naming observations

- **"MatOS"** = mat + OS. That's descriptive and memorable, and it supports the "operating system for your gym" story.
- **Ambiguities:**
  - Capitalisation varies: "MatOS" in docs and the manifest, "matOS" in the brief, "MATOS" in the UI because Bebas Neue is caps-only.
  - Spoken, it can be heard as "mattos" (a common Portuguese/Brazilian surname). That's a neutral or even positive BJJ association, but it's worth checking.
  - The "OS" may read as technical to some owners.
- **Practical checks not done:** trademark search (IE/EU/US/BR), domain availability, App Store name availability, social handles.

## 9. Visual identity directions (options, none chosen)

| Direction                    | Ingredients                                                                                                            | Signals                           |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **A. Belt system**           | Belt colours as the secondary palette; stripe dots as a motif; the belt chip as the core icon language; Bebas headings | Instantly BJJ; progression        |
| **B. Tatami / mat grid**     | Square mark built from mat tiles; a subtle grid texture; navy + off-white + one warm accent (gold/brown belt tone)     | Structure, calm, "OS"             |
| **C. Fight-poster heritage** | Condensed caps, bold contrast, photography of real training, numbered "rounds" for steps                               | Energy, competition, community    |
| **D. Quiet technical**       | Mostly neutral, precise data visuals, navy anchor, minimal ornament                                                    | Trust, analysis, premium software |

**Colours that could suit:**

- Deep navy (existing; trust).
- The belt colours used meaningfully (white / blue / purple / brown / black, plus kids belts).
- A warm accent such as brown-belt tan or a coral-red "tap".
- Off-white mats.

**Styles to avoid:**

- Generic fitness neon (lime/black "gym bro").
- Aggressive MMA imagery (blood, cage).
- Clip-art martial-arts silhouettes.
- Stock "smiling at laptop" photography.
- Rainbow dashboards that clash with belt colours.
- Using belt colours for status (red ≠ a belt; purple ≠ "warning").

## 10. Brand differentiators to lean on

1. BJJ-native progression (readiness, grading day, kids belts) shown visually through the belt.
2. The assistant as a named, characterful helper. Its naming and voice are a brand decision.
3. Athlete analysis and share cards: the brand travels on athletes' social posts.
4. Privacy by design (video never uploaded; opponent redaction).
5. Fair pricing story (if confirmed): free to start, fees only when money moves.
